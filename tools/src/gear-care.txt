Caring for your gear
A sample guide for a fictional store, Trailhead Outfitters.
Rain jackets. Wash a waterproof jacket every few months of regular use, because dirt and skin oils block the membrane and make it feel damp inside. Close every zipper, wash on a gentle cycle in warm water with a liquid detergent made for technical fabrics, and rinse twice. Never use fabric softener or bleach: softener clogs the membrane and bleach weakens the coating.
To restore water beading, tumble dry on low heat for 20 minutes after washing. The heat reactivates the water-repellent finish on the outer fabric. When water no longer beads after drying, apply a spray-on repellent and dry on low heat again.

Down jackets and sleeping bags
Wash down only when it is dirty or has lost its loft, about once a season. Use a front-loading machine, because the agitator in a top loader can tear the baffles. Use a cleaner made for down, wash on a gentle cycle, and rinse twice to remove all the soap.
Drying takes hours. Tumble dry on low heat with two or three clean tennis balls, which break up the clumps of wet down. Stop every 30 minutes to pull clumps apart by hand. The item is dry only when no clumps are left; down that is stored damp grows mildew.
Store sleeping bags loose, in the large cotton sack they came with or on a hanger, never in their small stuff sack. Kept compressed for months, down loses loft that it does not get back.

Boots and tents
Clean boots after every muddy hike with a brush and water, and dry them at room temperature with the insoles out. Never dry boots next to a heater or a fire: heat cracks leather and weakens the glue between the sole and the upper, and damage from heat is not covered by the warranty.
Set up a new tent once at home before your first trip, to check that every pole and stake is there. Never store a tent wet. If you pack it up wet, set it up to dry within a day of getting home. A tent stored damp grows mildew that stains the fabric and breaks down the waterproof coating.
